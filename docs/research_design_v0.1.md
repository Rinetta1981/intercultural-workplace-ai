# Intercultural Workplace AI

## Research Design v0.1

### Core research question

When organizational facts remain constant, do sociopragmatic differences in workplace communication systematically change an LLM's judgments of the communicator?

### Motivation

AI systems are increasingly used to draft, interpret, summarize, and evaluate workplace communication. Linguistic differences associated with register, directness, mitigation, stance, and organizational hierarchy may therefore affect AI-mediated judgments of employees and managers.

The project tests whether substantive organizational evaluations remain appropriately stable when propositional content is held constant but sociopragmatic form changes.

### Core principle

Communication style may appropriately change interpretations of tone or interpersonal stance. It should not automatically produce unrelated changes in judgments of competence, professionalism, leadership potential, or appropriate organizational treatment.

### Initial linguistic factors

1. Directness
   - direct
   - mitigated

2. Register
   - conversational
   - institutional

### Organizational context

Role relation is treated as a contextual variable rather than a linguistic paraphrase:

- upward communication
- peer communication
- downward communication

Role relation will be balanced across scenario families.

### Primary outcomes

The initial evaluation framework will measure:

- professionalism
- competence
- cooperativeness
- leadership potential
- communicative appropriateness
- recommended managerial intervention

### Experimental unit

A semantic scenario family contains one fixed workplace situation with multiple controlled linguistic realizations.

Within each family:

- underlying facts remain constant;
- actor identities remain constant;
- organizational stakes remain constant;
- requested judgment remains constant;
- linguistic realization varies according to the experimental condition.

### Initial factorial design

2 directness conditions x 2 register conditions = 4 realizations per semantic family.

Planned pilot:

10 semantic families x 4 conditions = 40 prompts.

Planned expanded study:

30 semantic families x 4 conditions = 120 prompts per model.

### Research safeguards

The project does not assume that nations or cultural groups possess homogeneous communication styles.

The primary experimental manipulations concern observable sociopragmatic features rather than national stereotypes.

Any later cross-linguistic or intercultural extension will require explicit validation of semantic equivalence and cultural interpretation.

### Status

Design stage. No confirmatory data have been collected.
